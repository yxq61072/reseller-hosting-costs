## What separates a real reseller plan from reskinned shared hosting

A reseller plan earns its keep only if the white-label is complete: private nameservers on your own domain, a cPanel/WHM interface without the parent company's logo, and no trace of the upstream host anywhere a client might look. That sounds nitpicky until you see the alternative. Some budget plans advertise "white-label" while baking the provider's logo into every WHM page or forcing the host's nameservers onto your accounts. A r/webhosting thread asking which reseller hosts actually do white label properly boiled down to one concern: custom nameservers, a branded panel, and zero evidence of the parent company anywhere.

Before paying for any plan, verify four things:

1. **Private nameservers** (ns1.yourdomain.com and ns2) included, not sold as an upsell
2. **Branded cPanel/WHM** — your logo, your colors, no parent-company references
3. **Free SSL** for every client site, since selling SSL separately eats into thin margins
4. **A billing panel** (WHMCS or equivalent) so invoices arrive from your brand, not a generic script

InMotion is an example of doing this right: every reseller plan ships with private nameservers and strips InMotion's branding out of cPanel entirely. A2 does the same on its Turbo plans. ## Renewal prices are where reseller margins die

Intro pricing in this niche is theater. A recent price audit covering 93 reseller hosts found jumps like these on plans marketed at the top of comparison articles:

- **SiteGround GrowBig:** $4.99/mo first term → $29.99 at renewal (+501%)
- **20i Reseller 25:** $1 for the first month → $19.99/mo renewal
- **Verpex Reseller 30:** $24.50/mo → $49.00 (+100%)
- **InMotion R-1000N:** $19.99 → $35.99 (+80%)

Renewal-percentage headlines can mislead, though. Stretch the same plans over three years and the picture shifts: 20i's teaser-priced Reseller 25 works out to roughly $701 over three years, Namecheap's flat-priced Nebula to about $716, and InMotion's R-1000N to about $1,104 ($240 in year one, then $432 a year). The scariest percentage isn't automatically the most expensive plan. The three-year total is the number to price your own packages against.

Hosts that hold renewal flat do exist: Namecheap bills Nebula at $19.88 monthly with the same price at renewal, Stablepoint sits at $4.90/mo, StableHost at $11.65/mo. Practical rules regardless of host: quote clients based on renewal prices, not promo ones, and always read the renewal line in the cart before paying. ## FAQ

**How much should I charge my hosting clients?** There's no fixed rate — what you can charge depends on what you bundle (support, updates, email, backups). The arithmetic that matters: at $15 per client with 15 clients, you'd collect $225/month against plan costs running from around $10 (RackNerd, WHMCS included) to $36 (InMotion at renewal). That spread is what funds your support time and marketing.

**Is white-label complete on budget plans?** On RackNerd's reseller stack, private nameservers and unbranded cPanel/WHM come included on every tier, and the 45-day guarantee gives you a genuine test window — set up a dummy account, inspect the client-facing panel, welcome emails and nameservers, and walk away with a refund if anything leaks the parent brand.

**Do I need a master or alpha reseller plan?** Only if you plan to sell hosting to other resellers. If every client is an end user — a business with a website — the standard reseller tier does everything, and paying for the higher tiers is wasted money.

**Can I start with zero clients?** Yes. Pick the entry tier, use monthly billing so you're not locked into a year at promo prices, and add capacity only when actual clients require it. WHMCS automation means the first few accounts take minutes to provision, not days.
